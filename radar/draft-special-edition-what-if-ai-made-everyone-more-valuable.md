# WHITE RABBIT RADAR · SPECIAL EDITION
## What If AI Made Everyone More Valuable?
**Status:** EDITORIAL DRAFT · NOT PUBLISHED
**Frontier:** Human Possibility / AI & Autonomy
**Draft date:** October 25, 2026
**Deck:** The first wave of AI asked what machines could do instead of us. What if the next wave asked what people could accomplish with them?

### The question nobody puts on the earnings slide
Picture two organizations introducing the same AI system. One calculates how many positions it can eliminate. The other asks how many employees it can help become designers, researchers, problem-solvers, mentors, and inventors.

Both might improve efficiency. Only one is deliberately investing in human possibility.

This isn't an argument that automation never displaces workers. It does, and AI may reshape careers in ways that are painful and uneven. It's an argument that **displacement is not the only outcome worth designing for**. A productivity gain is a number. What people get to *do* with that gain is a choice.

### When experience becomes shareable
In a study published in *The Quarterly Journal of Economics* in 2025, researchers examined the introduction of a generative-AI assistant among **5,172 customer-support agents** at one business-software company, studied through a staggered rollout. On average, agents with access resolved **15% more issues per hour**. Less-experienced and lower-skilled workers gained more than their highly experienced colleagues. The researchers also found evidence of learning, more polite customer interactions, and fewer requests for a supervisor.

The intriguing mechanism is not simply faster typing. A system trained on past conversations can expose a newer worker to patterns of expertise that previously took months of experience to acquire. In that setting, AI helped distribute know-how.

But there is an important wrinkle: the highest-skilled agents saw little benefit and small quality declines on some measures. Augmentation is not universal. The tool has to fit the task and the person. This study did not establish wage or overall employment effects.

**Source:** Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, “Generative AI at Work,” *QJE* (2025), https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044

### What would you do with 40% of your time back?
In a randomized experiment published in *Science* in 2023, **453 college-educated professionals** completed occupational writing tasks. Participants using ChatGPT spent, on average, **40% less time completing the writing tasks**, with assessed quality **18% higher**.

These are task-level findings from an experiment, not proof that every professional can reclaim 40% of a workweek. But they make the bigger question tangible: if routine drafting becomes less demanding, does the worker get more time to think, learn, challenge assumptions, or design something better? Or does the organization simply raise the output quota?

**Source:** Noy & Zhang, “Experimental evidence on the productivity effects of generative artificial intelligence,” *Science* (2023), https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2586

### A tool is not a transformation
A field experiment involving **7,137 knowledge workers across 66 firms** tested generative AI embedded in everyday workplace applications. In the second half of the six-month experiment, the estimated reduction among tool users—80% of those offered access—was about **two email hours weekly**, alongside less time working outside regular hours. Beyond these time savings, the researchers did **not** detect shifts in the quantity or composition of measured tasks from providing individuals access alone.

That's the hinge of this edition. Installing software is not the same as redesigning opportunity. The second step requires management decisions: time for learning, permission to experiment, access to expertise, new career paths, and incentives that reward employees for making systems better.

**Source:** Dillon, Jaffe, Immorlica & Stanton, “Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI,” NBER Working Paper 33795 (2025), November 13, 2025 revision: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11436v4

### When expertise crosses a boundary
A field experiment published in *Organization Science* in June 2026 tested AI on real product-development challenges at Procter & Gamble. Among 791 participating professionals, individuals using AI produced proposals of comparable assessed quality to two-person teams without it. Their proposals also combined commercial and technical perspectives more evenly.

That is a concrete example of expertise becoming more accessible. It is not evidence that those proposals became successful products, or that a company can dispense with human teams. The experiment measured proposal quality in a particular setting, not long-term commercial results.

**Source:** Dell’Acqua and colleagues, “The Cybernetic Teammate: A Field Experiment on Generative AI and Teamwork,” *Organization Science* (published online June 12, 2026), https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.20702. The published paper reports 791 participants; 776 supplied complete data including post-task surveys.

### A retraining decision in the real world
Ingka Group, the largest IKEA retailer, reported in June 2023 that it had reskilled 8,500 call-centre workers in remote interior design, digital sales, relationship-building and complex customer support while its Billie chatbot handled simpler enquiries.

That documents an employer choosing to invest in different skills as automation changes the work. It does not independently establish that all 8,500 workers moved into permanent designer jobs, received higher pay, or gained lasting job security. Those worker outcomes still need reporting.

**Source:** Ingka Group, “AI and Remote Selling bring IKEA design expertise to the many” (June 2023), https://www.ingka.com/newsroom/ai-and-remote-selling-bring-ikea-design-expertise-to-the-many/ (employer-reported programme, not an independent outcomes evaluation).

### The opportunity beyond the office
Imagine a maintenance technician who has spent twenty years noticing a recurring equipment failure but never had the tools to model a solution. Or a caregiver with a clever device idea, but no engineering department. Or a small-business owner who knows precisely which process wastes everyone's time.

AI does not turn a sketch into a safe, market-ready product by magic. Real invention still requires validation, expertise, materials, testing, funding, and sometimes regulatory approval. But lowering the cost of research, drafting, coding, visualization, and iteration could allow more people to *begin*.

One named example is entrepreneur Pieter Levels, who launched Photo AI in February 2023. The service uses models trained on uploaded photographs to generate personalised images. In his launch account, Levels described roughly two months of development, input from fellow makers, and human testing.

AI supplies the image-generation capability; the product still needed development, interface decisions, testing, hosting and a business around it. This is an experienced entrepreneur building with AI, not a demonstration that AI independently invented a business or that anyone can reproduce his results. His account does not provide an independently audited breakdown of costs or financial returns.

**Sources:** Levels’ first-person launch account, https://levels.io/photoai-photorealistic-ai-photo-studio; live product, https://photoai.com/. The technician, caregiver and small-business examples above remain hypothetical.

### The uncomfortable questions
Who keeps the value when an employee becomes 15% more productive? Is the reward better pay, shorter hours, a richer job, or simply more work? Will organizations provide training to the people most vulnerable to displacement? Could constant AI assistance weaken judgment or make workers dependent on systems they cannot inspect? Can workers challenge automated recommendations?

There is already evidence that time savings need not become higher pay. In the April 15, 2025 version of a Danish study, Anders Humlum and Emilie Vestergaard linked worker surveys to administrative employment records. They reported modest time savings from AI chatbots but no significant effects on earnings or recorded hours over the period studied. That does not show that employers captured every dollar saved: the study does not measure the complete division of gains among workers, employers, customers and AI suppliers.

**Source:** Humlum & Vestergaard, “Large Language Models, Small Labor Market Effects,” April 15, 2025 working-paper version, https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BFI_WP_2025-56-1.pdf. This is an early observation window, not a verdict on later effects; the paper has subsequently been revised and retitled.

Newer U.S. evidence also makes the risks concrete. An August 2026 Stanford working paper, using ADP payroll data through June 2026, found that employment of workers aged 22–25 in AI-exposed occupations stood 19% below where it would have been had it kept pace with less-exposed peers. The gap operated mainly through reduced hiring. The authors found no evidence of widespread, economy-wide displacement and explicitly described the findings as descriptive indicators, not causal estimates of AI job losses. Pre-existing trends and differences across datasets complicate interpretation.

**Source:** Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, “Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence,” August 2026 version, https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf.

An optimistic future doesn't require pretending those questions are solved. It requires asking them early enough to shape the answers.

The research so far demonstrates **specific productivity and learning benefits under specific conditions**. It does not establish that AI automatically increases wages, job security, autonomy, or well-being. Those outcomes depend on institutions, access, worker voice, and choices.

### The humanity of humanity
The extraordinary thing about human beings is not that we can perform repetitive tasks without error. We can't. It is that we can imagine an alternative, recognize a problem nobody assigned us, care about a stranger, and make something that didn't exist yesterday.

What if the measure of successful AI adoption were not just *how much work disappeared*, but **how much human possibility appeared**?

A new skill. A new invention. A person who finally has the tools to pursue an idea. A workplace where experience is shared rather than hoarded.

The future is already leaking through.

**Chase the extraordinary.**
