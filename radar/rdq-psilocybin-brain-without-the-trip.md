## Two-sentence synopsis
A 2026 mouse study finds that psilocybin-induced dendritic spine remodeling depends on serotonin 2A signaling in layer 5 cortical pyramidal neurons, while a mouse behavioral proxy for psychedelic effects can be experimentally separated from that remodeling. The result points toward possible non-hallucinogenic psychoplastogens, but neither clinical efficacy nor a hallucination-free antidepressant treatment has been established.

## Initial fact-check
1. Living mouse brain imaging showed new dendritic spines forming within 24 hours, with accelerated maturation.
2. Psilocybin also removed some older synapses; 'more connections' alone is misleading.
3. Chronic-stress mouse models showed a different remodeling pattern from controls, including protection from excessive spine loss.
4. Cell-specific 5-HT2A manipulations dissociated structural remodeling from the mouse head-twitch response. Head twitch is a proxy, not direct measurement of subjective hallucinations.
5. Structural plasticity is not synonymous with antidepressant benefit; animal findings cannot establish human clinical outcomes.

## Deep-dive questions
- Reproduce study design, sample sizes, effect sizes, time course, statistical uncertainty and limitations from primary paper.
- What makes new spines functional and durable, and does their location matter?
- Which cortical circuits were studied, and how does stress alter the response?
- Is the subjective psychedelic experience required for clinical efficacy in humans? Compare conflicting evidence.
- Compare psilocybin, ketamine, 5-MeO-DMT, and non-hallucinogenic psychoplastogen candidates.
- Contrast structural two-photon imaging, human fMRI, EEG and neurofeedback: what does each actually measure?
- Explore targeted plasticity and activity-dependent rewiring without suggesting thought-controlled DNA modification is established.
- Evaluate adverse effects, mania/psychosis screening, trial eligibility, durability, access and regulation.
- Identify researchers worth interviewing: Yi Zuo, Jacob Baker, Alex Kwan.
- Find human randomized trials and negative/replication evidence.

## Editorial guardrails
No recommendation to self-medicate. Avoid 'brain repair' as proven clinical outcome, 'hallucination-free treatment exists', or 'mouse head twitch equals human trip'. Clearly label animal vs human data and hypotheses.
