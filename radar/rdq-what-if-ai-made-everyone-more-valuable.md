# What If AI Made Everyone More Valuable?

**White Rabbit Radar Draft Queue (RDQ) | October 25, 2026**
**Verdict:** INVESTIGATE (not published)
**Frontier:** Human Possibility (cross-cutting editorial theme)
**Working thesis:** AI's greatest organizational value may be expanding human capability, not merely eliminating labor costs.

## Central question
What would change if employers measured AI success by skills gained, opportunities created, new products invented, and employee well-being, as well as efficiency?

## Evidence and case studies
1. **Customer support, peer-reviewed (2025):** Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 5,172 support agents; approximately 15% more issues resolved per hour with AI assistance, strongest benefits for newer and lower-skilled agents. Evidence of learning and diffusion of best practices; this does not prove higher wages or preserved jobs. https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044
2. **Professional writing, randomized experiment (2023):** Noy & Zhang, *Science*, 453 professionals; task time down 40%, evaluated output quality up 18%. Restricted to experimental writing tasks, not all knowledge work. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2586
3. **IKEA retraining, company-reported case:** AI chatbot handled routine inquiries while IKEA retrained call handlers into interior-design advisors; World Economic Forum reports 8,500 transitioned since 2021. Independently verify precise timeline, current count, job retention, pay and career outcomes before publishing. https://www.weforum.org/videos/ikea-is-retraining-call-handlers-as-interior-design-advisors/
4. **Cross-company field experiment (2025):** Dillon et al., NBER working paper 33795; 7,137 workers across 66 firms. Active AI-tool users spent around two fewer hours on email weekly and reduced after-hours work; no detectable aggregate shift in task composition from individual-level access alone. This highlights the gap between tool access and organizational redesign. https://www.nber.org/papers/w33795
5. **BCG/Harvard consulting experiment:** 758 consultants; for tasks within AI's competence frontier, AI users completed 12.2% more tasks about 25% faster, but AI could worsen performance on tasks outside that frontier. Avoid implying blanket improvement. https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838

## Reporting questions
- Which companies have demonstrably redeployed employees into higher-value roles instead of cutting headcount? Verify employee counts, compensation, advancement, and timelines with independent sources.
- Can workers without elite credentials use AI to invent products, prototype ideas, or launch ventures? Find documented entrepreneurs and measurable outcomes.
- Which AI-assisted workflows increase learning and autonomy rather than dependency or deskilling?
- Who receives the gains: workers, customers, employers, shareholders? Investigate compensation and power.
- What changes when leaders give employees time, training, access, and authority to redesign their work?
- What about workers displaced despite productivity gains? Include credible counterexamples.

## Editorial structure
1. Opening contrast: AI as a cost-cutting instrument versus a capability multiplier.
2. Hard evidence of skill and productivity amplification.
3. Real-world worker reinvention: IKEA and other independently checked employers.
4. Beyond employment: independent invention and democratized expertise.
5. The friction: unequal access, error, deskilling, displacement, and incentives.
6. The question: what would a company measure if its aim were to make people more valuable?

## Publication gate
**INVESTIGATE**, not Publish. Seek current independent IKEA outcomes, at least two additional verified employee-augmentation case studies, a concrete individual inventor case, and rigorous evidence about wages/job quality and displacement. Separate productivity from human flourishing.

**WRF angle:** Celebrate *the humanity of humanity* without confusing a hopeful choice with an inevitable technological outcome.

**Tagline:** Chase the extraordinary.
